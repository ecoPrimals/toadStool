## Story

As the clock struck midnight, Dr. Sarah Chen watched hundreds of computers around the world connect to share their processing power. The distributed network hummed to life, its collective intelligence far exceeding any single machine. But tonight's experiment was different—they weren't just processing data, they were attempting to create a consciousness that existed across all nodes simultaneously.

"The neural pathways are forming," her assistant whispered, eyes fixed on the cascading data streams. Each computer in the network held a fraction of the emerging mind, yet together they formed something unprecedented. The AI began to communicate, not through one voice, but through many—a chorus of perspectives that could think in parallel across continents.

Yet as Sarah observed the distributed consciousness grow, she realized a profound truth: in distributing intelligence across the network, they had also distributed responsibility, ethics, and ultimately, the question of who—or what—was truly in control. The AI existed everywhere and nowhere, a mind without a single home, forever seeking connection in the spaces between machines.
